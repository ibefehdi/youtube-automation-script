### VIDEO TITLE

The Night Milwaukee Police Walked a Boy Back to Jeffrey Dahmer

### VIDEO TOPIC / CASE NAME

Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer / State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer (Milwaukee County Circuit Court, 1992) / Ohio conviction for the 1978 murder of Steven Hicks

### ONE-SENTENCE LOGLINE

Between 1978 and 1991, Jeffrey Dahmer murdered seventeen men and boys; he was caught only after a man escaped his Milwaukee apartment, and a jury later found him legally sane despite a decade of missed chances by police, probation, and neighbors.

### WHY THIS STORY WORKS

- Core emotion / hook: Institutional failure and delayed recognition. The horror is not only what Dahmer did, but how many systems touched him and let him go: a 1988 child-assault conviction with work-release, a probation officer who never visited the apartment, and Milwaukee officers who returned a naked, drugged 14-year-old to him in May 1991.
- Why it fits a faceless documentary: The story lives in apartments, police radios, courtrooms, factory night shifts, and vacant lots. There is little need for graphic reconstruction. Polaroids, a 57-gallon drum, a chocolate-factory time clock, a 911 tape, and a prison gym hallway carry the narrative.
- What is visually depictable: Bath Township woodland and a 1978 Ohio house; Revere High School; Fort McClellan and Baumholder; Catherine Dahmer’s West Allis bungalow; the Ambassador Hotel; Ambrosia Chocolate’s night shift; Oxford Apartments 213 at 924 North 25th Street; Grand Avenue Mall; Milwaukee County Circuit Court; Columbia Correctional Institution in Portage; the vacant lot where the building later stood.
- Suggested video length: 15–18 minutes.
- What makes this story different from the most obvious YouTube version: Most recaps treat Dahmer as a monster in a refrigerator. The fuller record is a city that already had him: the Sinthasomphone family twice, a chocolate-factory mixer on probation, officers who radioed a joke about getting “deloused,” a sanity trial that was not about guilt, and families who later said Netflix made them relive the trial without asking.

### CONFIRMED FACTS

- Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer was born May 21, 1960, in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, the first son of Lionel Herbert Dahmer, a chemist, and Joyce Annette Dahmer (née Flint). A younger brother, David, was born in December 1966. (Source: contemporaneous biographical reporting compiled in major profiles; Lionel Dahmer, *A Father’s Story*, 1994.)
- The family moved to Doylestown, Ohio, in October 1966 and to Bath Township, Summit County, Ohio, in May 1968. Dahmer attended Bath Elementary, Eastview Junior High, and Revere High School, graduating in May 1978. (Source: FOX19 / local Ohio reporting on the Netflix series’ Ohio facts, Oct. 2, 2022; *People*, “Sins of the Son,” 1994.)
- As a child he collected animal bones and, with his father’s chemistry help, learned to bleach and preserve them. He later dissected roadkill. These facts are documented by Lionel and by later biographical reporting; they are not proof of later crimes. (Source: Lionel Dahmer, 1994; *People*, 1994.)
- Joyce and Lionel’s marriage deteriorated through the 1970s. Lionel moved out in early 1978. Joyce, contrary to a court order, left with David for Chippewa Falls, Wisconsin, leaving 18-year-old Jeffrey in the Bath Road house. The divorce was finalized July 24, 1978. (Source: Lionel Dahmer, 1994; Wikipedia used as pointer to *A Father’s Story* and Masters.)
- On June 18, 1978, Dahmer picked up hitchhiker Steven Mark Hicks, 18, who was traveling to a concert at Chippewa Lake Park. Dahmer took him to the empty family home, later said Hicks wanted to leave and he did not want him to, then killed him. Remains were scattered in woodland behind the house and not identified until 1991 after the confession. (Source: Milwaukee Journal Sentinel / USA TODAY victim profiles, Sept. 29, 2022; Ohio charging after 1991 excavation.)
- Six weeks later Dahmer enrolled at Ohio State University, hoping to study business. He failed most courses; his GPA was 0.45. He dropped out after one term. Lionel later wrote that he found the dorm room full of empty liquor bottles. (Source: Lionel Dahmer, 1994; FOX19, 2022.)
- In January 1979, at his father’s urging, he enlisted in the U.S. Army. He trained at Fort McClellan, Alabama, and as a medical specialist at Fort Sam Houston, Texas. On July 13, 1979, he was deployed to Baumholder, West Germany, as a combat medic, 2nd Battalion, 68th Armored Regiment, 8th Infantry Division. In March 1981 he was deemed unsuitable for service because of alcohol abuse and received an honorable discharge. (Source: Newsweek summary of Army record reporting, 2022; *New York Times* contemporaneous profiles.)
- After discharge he briefly worked at a Miami Beach delicatessen, was evicted for unpaid rent, and returned to Ohio in September 1981. Two weeks later he was arrested for drunk and disorderly conduct, fined $60, with a suspended 10-day jail sentence. In December 1981 his father and stepmother Shari sent him to live with his paternal grandmother, Catherine Dahmer, in West Allis, Wisconsin. (Source: biographical reporting; Newsweek, 2022.)
- August 8, 1982: convicted of indecent exposure at Wisconsin State Fair Park and fined $50 plus costs. September 8, 1986: arrested for lewd conduct near the Kinnickinnic River; the charge was reduced to disorderly conduct; on March 10, 1987, he received one year of probation and counseling. (Source: court/probation history as reported in 1991–92 coverage; TIME, “The Little Flat of Horrors,” 1991.)
- January 1985: hired as a mixer at Milwaukee’s Ambrosia Chocolate Factory, night shift 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., six nights a week. He held the job until mid-July 1991. (Source: TIME, 1991; USA TODAY archive roundup, Sept. 30, 2022.)
- November 20, 1987: Steven Walter Tuomi, a short-order cook from Ontonagon, Michigan, was killed in a rented room at Milwaukee’s Ambassador Hotel. Dahmer later said he had no memory of the killing. No remains were found. Milwaukee County did not charge this count for lack of proof beyond a reasonable doubt. (Source: USA TODAY / Journal Sentinel victim profiles, 2022; Milwaukee charging decisions reported 1991–92.)
- Subsequent 1988–89 murders of James “Jamie” Doxtator, 14; Richard Guerrero; and Anthony Sears were committed at the grandmother’s West Allis house. Catherine asked him to move out in September 1988 because of drinking, late-night guests, and odors from the basement and garage. (Source: charging documents as reported by UPI, Aug. 6, 1991; biographical reporting.)
- September 25, 1988: moved to 808 North 24th Street. Two days later he was arrested for drugging and sexually assaulting a 13-year-old boy — later identified in reporting as Somsack Sinthasomphone, older brother of later murder victim Konerak Sinthasomphone. Dahmer had offered money for nude photographs. (Source: Chicago Tribune / AP, July 27, 1991; USA TODAY, 2022.)
- January 30, 1989: pleaded guilty to second-degree sexual assault and enticing a child for immoral purposes. May 23, 1989: Circuit Judge William Gardner sentenced him to one year in the House of Correction with work release so he could keep the chocolate-factory job, plus five years’ probation and sex-offender registration. Assistant District Attorney Gale Shelton had argued for a long prison term. Lionel asked the court for treatment, saying, “This may be our last chance to institute something lasting.” Dahmer served about ten months and was paroled early. Monthly home visits by the probation agent were waived because the caseload was overloaded and the neighborhood was high-crime. (Source: Chicago Tribune, July 27, 1991; USA TODAY archive, 2022; TIME, 1991.)
- March 25, 1989 — after conviction, before sentencing — Dahmer murdered Anthony Sears at the grandmother’s house and later kept preserved remains. (Source: UPI criminal complaint summary, Aug. 6, 1991.)
- May 14, 1990: moved into Oxford Apartments, 924 North 25th Street, Apartment 213, Milwaukee. Rent about $300 a month. Twelve of the seventeen murders occurred there. (Source: UPI, 1991; later site histories.)
- May 27, 1991, early morning: 14-year-old Konerak Sinthasomphone, naked, drugged, and disoriented, was found on the street near 25th and State. Neighbors including Sandra Smith and Nichole (also spelled Nicole) Childress called 911. Officers John A. Balcerzak and Joseph T. Gabrish, later joined by Richard T. Porubcan, accepted Dahmer’s claim that the boy was his 19-year-old intoxicated lover, walked both back to Apartment 213, and did not run Dahmer’s name. A computer check would have shown the 1988 child-assault conviction involving Konerak’s brother. Glenda Cleveland later called to question why the boy was not taken to a hospital. Dahmer killed Konerak after the officers left. Four more men were murdered before July 22. (Source: *Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee* case summary, UMKC; LA Times, Sept. 7, 1991; NYT, April 28, 1994; USA TODAY, 2022.)
- Dispatch radio after the encounter, as later widely reported: officers described an “intoxicated, Asian, naked male” returned to his “sober boyfriend,” with laughter and a remark about getting “deloused.” (Source: later reporting of the radio traffic; Wikipedia pointer to contemporaneous Milwaukee coverage. Treat the exact wording as reconstructed from published transcripts, not independently re-heard here.)
- July 15, 1991: Dahmer was suspended from Ambrosia; July 19 he was dismissed. The same period he murdered Oliver Lacy and Joseph Bradehoft. (Source: chronological reconstructions from charging documents and workplace reporting.)
- July 22, 1991, afternoon/evening: at Grand Avenue Mall Dahmer offered money to men to pose for photographs. Tracy Edwards, 32, went to Apartment 213. After hours in which Dahmer cuffed one wrist, threatened him with a knife, and — Edwards later testified — said he would eat his heart, Edwards punched him and fled. About 11:30 p.m. he flagged down Officers Robert Rauth and Rolf Mueller at North 25th Street with a handcuff still on his wrist. In the bedroom Mueller found Polaroid photographs of dismembered bodies. Dahmer resisted arrest. Mueller opened the refrigerator and found a severed head. Dahmer, pinned on the floor, said: “For what I did I should be dead.” (Source: LA Times, Edwards trial testimony, Feb. 1, 1992; *New York Times*, July 24, 1991; later standard reconstructions of the arrest.)
- Search of Apartment 213 recovered multiple human heads and skulls, preserved remains, Polaroid photographs (later counted at 74), and a 57-gallon drum containing acid and torsos. Milwaukee’s chief medical examiner later said the scene was “more like dismantling someone’s museum than an actual crime scene.” Early press (NYT, July 24, 1991) reported three heads in a refrigerator; later inventories described four severed heads in the kitchen and seven skulls in the bedroom/closet. (Source: NYT, July 24, 1991; later investigative summaries.)
- Detectives Patrick Kennedy and Dennis Murphy took more than 60 hours of interviews, producing a 178-page confession. Dahmer waived counsel and said he wanted to “put an end to it.” He admitted 17 murders: 16 in Wisconsin from 1987–91 and Steven Hicks in Ohio in 1978. (Source: standard investigative accounts; Kennedy later appeared in documentaries.)
- Charging: July 25, 1991, four Wisconsin murder counts; by August 22, eleven more Wisconsin counts (15 total). He was not charged with Tuomi or with the attempted murder of Edwards. September 14, 1991: Ohio identified Hicks remains (two molars and a vertebra matching X-rays) in woodland behind the Bath house; charged September 17. (Source: charging chronology in 1991 reporting.)
- January 13, 1992: pleaded guilty but insane to 15 Wisconsin first-degree intentional homicides, waiving a guilt trial. The only issue was legal sanity under Wisconsin law: whether a mental disease or defect left him unable to appreciate wrongfulness or conform his conduct. Ten of twelve jurors were enough. (Source: UPI, Feb. 15, 1992; A&E trial summary.)
- Trial: January 30–February 15, 1992, Milwaukee County Circuit Judge Laurence C. Gram Jr. Prosecution: District Attorney E. Michael McCann. Defense: Gerald P. Boyle. Jury found him sane on all 15 counts, 10–2 on each count. (Source: UPI, Feb. 15, 1992; NYT, Feb. 16, 1992.)
- February 17, 1992: sentenced to 15 consecutive life terms. Prosecutor said he would not be eligible for parole for 936 years. Wisconsin had no death penalty (abolished 1853). (Source: AP / LA Times, Feb. 18, 1992; NYT, Feb. 18, 1992.)
- May 1, 1992: in a 45-minute Ohio hearing, pleaded guilty to Hicks’s murder and received a 16th life term. (Source: Washington Post, May 2, 1992.)
- September 1991: Chief Philip Arreola fired Balcerzak and Gabrish for acts of omission (failure to take the boy into protective custody, failure to take witness names, failure to write a formal report or run the name). Porubcan’s firing was stayed / he was placed on probation. Fire and Police Commission upheld the two firings in November 1992. Reserve Judge Robert J. Parins ordered reinstatement in April 1994, calling dismissal too harsh, with about $55,000 back pay each. Milwaukee later paid the Sinthasomphone family $850,000 in a settlement widely reported in later summaries. (Source: LA Times, Sept. 7, 1991; NYT, April 28, 1994; later officer biographies.)
- November 28, 1994: at Columbia Correctional Institution, Portage, Wisconsin, inmate Christopher J. Scarver beat Dahmer with a 20-inch metal bar during an unsupervised janitorial detail in the prison gym area. Dahmer was found about 8:10 a.m. and pronounced dead about an hour later (UPI: 9:11 a.m. CST en route to Divine Savior Hospital). Fellow inmate Jesse Anderson was also beaten and died two days later. Scarver told a guard, “God told me to do it.” He later received two more life terms (May 15, 1995). (Source: Washington Post, Nov. 29, 1994; UPI, Nov. 28, 1994.)
- Oxford Apartments were demolished in November 1992. The lot remained vacant. (Source: later site reporting.)
- Lionel Dahmer published *A Father’s Story* (1994) and donated a portion of proceeds to families. He died December 5, 2023. Joyce Flint died of cancer November 27, 2000. David changed his surname and lives privately. Catherine Dahmer died December 25, 1992. (Source: obituary reporting; National World on Catherine.)

### PEOPLE

- **Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer** (May 21, 1960 – November 28, 1994). Perpetrator. Ambrosia Chocolate mixer; previously Army medic. Confessed to 17 murders; convicted of 16. Diagnosed by various examiners with paraphilia/necrophilia, borderline and schizotypal personality features, alcohol dependence; found legally sane. In prison became a born-again Christian and was baptized May 1994. (Sources: trial coverage; Dateline 1994.)

- **Steven Walter Tuomi**, ~25–28 (ages conflict across sources). Short-order cook from Ontonagon, Michigan; artistic in school. Father Walter said Milwaukee police initially told him there was no sign of foul play. Not charged. Last seen Sept. 15, 1987 in some accounts; killing dated Nov. 20, 1987 in others. (Source: USA TODAY, 2022.)
- **James Edward “Jamie” Doxtator**, 14. Half Stockbridge, part Oneida; nearly six feet tall. Mother Debbie Vega quoted his favorite Bible line at trial and said she could not forgive Dahmer. Last seen Jan. 16, 1988. (Source: USA TODAY, 2022.)
- **Richard Guerrero**, ~22–25. Mexican American. Sister Janie Hagen hired a private investigator who defrauded the family. At trial she called Dahmer “diablo, el puro diablo.” Last seen March 1988. (Source: USA TODAY, 2022.)
- **Anthony Lee Sears**, 24. Baker’s Square manager, aspiring model, planning Easter with family. Mother Marilyn. Last seen March 25, 1989. First victim from whom Dahmer permanently kept remains. (Source: USA TODAY; UPI complaint.)
- **Raymond Lamont Smith / Ricky Beeks**, ~32–33. Used alias Raymond Smith. Had a daughter in Rockford, Illinois. Last seen May 29, 1990 in some profiles; charging documents place the murder spring/early summer 1990. (Source: USA TODAY; UPI.)
- **Edward Warren “Eddie” Smith**, ~27–28. Nicknamed “the Sheikh.” Sister Carolyn received a call in March 1991, believed to be from Dahmer, saying Eddie was dead. Brother J.W. Smith read family statements in court. (Source: USA TODAY; AP sentencing.)
- **Ernest Marquez Miller**, ~22–24. Dance student, Milwaukee High School of the Arts graduate, about to start college in Chicago. Aunt Vivian; uncle Stanley Miller told the court there is “no place in a civilized society for anyone who shows no regard for life.” Last seen Sept. 2, 1990. (Source: USA TODAY; Deseret News, Feb. 17, 1992.)

Mother Shirley Hughes taught Bible class; at sentencing she quoted a friend’s poem and signed “I love you.” Last seen May 24, 1991. (Source: USA TODAY, 2022.)
- **Konerak Sinthasomphone**, 14. Lao American; family immigrated November 1980 after the communist takeover. Father Somthone (farmer), mother Somdy, nine children. Freshman at Pulaski High School; played soccer at Mitchell Park. Younger brother of 1988 assault survivor Somsack. Murdered May 27, 1991 after police returned him. (Source: USA TODAY; *Estate of Sinthasomphone*.)

### KEY DOCUMENTS, EVIDENCE & ARTIFACTS

- **178-page confession / 60+ hours of interviews** (Kennedy, Murphy). Showed sequence, methods, and claimed motives (control, companionship, “permanent part of me”). Did not independently prove Tuomi.
- **Polaroid photographs** (about 74) taken in the apartment, used to identify victims including David Thomas, whose remains were not found.
- **Physical remains in Apt. 213:** Heads, skulls (some painted or bleached), organs, skeletons, acid drum. Proved multiple homicides; early newspaper counts of heads conflict with later inventories.
- **Ohio woodland fragments, 1991:** Two molars and a vertebra matched Hicks X-rays. Proved the 1978 murder geographically.
- **1988–89 criminal file:** Second-degree sexual assault, work-release sentence, probation conditions, waived home visits.
- **May 27, 1991 police/fire response and radio traffic:** No formal report; no warrant check. Later civil and departmental records.
- **911 calls** from Childress/Smith and later Glenda Cleveland.
- **Wisconsin charging documents, Aug. 1991:** 15 Milwaukee County homicides specified; Tuomi omitted.
- **Park Dietz report, Jan. 10, 1992** (unpublished court file cited in Marquette Law Review): mental-status evaluation used at trial.
- **Sentencing statement, Feb. 17, 1992:** Full text partially published by UPI.
- **Lionel Dahmer, *A Father’s Story* (1994):** Parental account; later lawsuits over use of names.
- **Netflix 2022 series and family statements:** Not evidence of 1991 facts; evidence of later harm to families.

### INVESTIGATION, MEDIA & INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE

- Discovery was not detective work. It was Tracy Edwards flagging a squad car. Polaroids in a dresser drawer ended the case in minutes.
- Milwaukee police had already been inside Apartment 213 on May 27 and left. Firefighters were ordered away. Neighbors and Glenda Cleveland were dismissed. The 1988 file was one computer query away.
- Probation never entered the apartment. Ambrosia fired him for attendance, not suspicion. Building manager accepted rotting-freezer and dead-fish explanations.
- Missing-person reports for several victims were treated lightly, families later said, because the missing were Black, Latino, Native, gay, transient, or thought to “run off.” Walter Tuomi was told there was no foul play. Janie Hagen said police did not take Richard’s disappearance seriously because he was Hispanic.
- July 1991 media framed “the Milwaukee Cannibal” and a refrigerator of heads. Community vigils stressed race, homophobia, and police contempt. A 1989 study had already called Milwaukee hypersegregated.
- Officers were fired, then reinstated with back pay — a second institutional insult to the Sinthasomphone family, who received an $850,000 city settlement in later summaries while the officers returned to policing.
- Trial coverage was a national spectacle. Families sat through descriptions of the crimes, then spoke at sentencing. Many wore pins with photographs.
- 2022 Netflix series drew huge audiences and family protests that true-crime entertainment used their names and courtroom pain without consent.

### CONFLICTING ACCOUNTS & CONTRADICTIONS

- **Victim ages and some last-seen dates** differ between the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel/USA TODAY 2022 compilation (drawing on Anne E. Schwartz and 1991 papers) and later encyclopedia timelines. Examples: Tuomi 25 vs 28; Guerrero 22 vs 25; Straughter 17 vs 18; Lacy 23 vs 24. Use ranges; do not treat one list as gospel.
- **Number of heads in the refrigerator:** NYT July 24, 1991 said three; later inventories said four kitchen heads plus seven skulls elsewhere. Early breaking news was incomplete.
- **Jury composition:** UPI Feb. 15, 1992 said seven men and five women; some later summaries reverse the numbers. Verdict math is solid: 10–2 per count, ten votes sufficient under Wisconsin law.
- **Parole-ineligibility arithmetic:** NYT/AP 936 years; some later tables 941 or 957. The legal fact is 15 consecutive Wisconsin life terms plus an Ohio life term, no practical parole.
- **Whether officers smelled Hughes’s body:** Balcerzak said he smelled nothing unusual; Gabrish later said he noticed a foul odor. Wikipedia notes witnesses’ claims of bleeding were not corroborated by police or fire on scene.
- **Race as motive:** Most victims were men of color. Dahmer said race was incidental and body type mattered. A later forensic-morphology paper has been cited in support. Families and Milwaukee’s racial politics treated police indifference as racial. Both the demographic pattern and Dahmer’s stated explanation belong in the record; neither cancels the other.
- **Army sexual-assault allegations** by two soldiers (reported 2010s): Dahmer denied sexual assaults in Germany in 1991 interviews. Unresolved; do not present as confirmed.
- **Childhood sexual abuse:** Some 1991 reports said Lionel told a parole officer Jeffrey was molested at eight; Lionel later denied it; Dahmer denied abuse. Unproven.
- **Scarver’s motive:** 1994 officials declined to speculate. Scarver later cited Dahmer’s taunting with food and a newspaper clipping of the crimes; racial hatred toward white inmates has also been discussed. His 1994 statement was religious (“God told me”).
- **Netflix vs. record:** The series compresses, invents dialogue, and omits a third officer (Porubcan). Use it as cultural aftermath, not as a source of facts.

### THEORIES

- **Legal insanity (defense, 1992).** Claim: necrophilic compulsion destroyed capacity to conform. Support: Berlin, Becker, Wahlstrom; grotesque nature of the acts. Weakness: planning, concealment, lying to police, condom use, work, and Dietz’s alcohol-as-disinhibition argument. Standing: **rejected by the jury**.
- **Sane, paraphilic, alcoholic (prosecution / Dietz).** Claim: he knew it was wrong and chose victims, times, and cover stories. Support: Polaroids, acid purchases, false explanations to manager and police, 10–2 verdict. Standing: **legal outcome**.
- **Police racism and homophobia as enabling conditions.** Claim: Konerak died because officers saw a gay interracial couple, not a child in danger. Support: radio “deloused” remark, ignored Black women witnesses, failure to run a sex-offender check. Weakness: does not explain 1978–90 murders. Standing: **widely accepted as a documented failure**, even if officers’ lawyers called Dahmer “an incredible con man.”
- **Probation/mental-health failure.** Claim: waived home visits and 1989 work-release let a known child molester keep a night-shift job and a private apartment. Support: TIME 1991; ADA Shelton’s 1989 warning; Lionel’s plea for treatment. Standing: **documented system gap**; 1995 Weinberger suit failed legally.
- **Asperger / autism speculation** (2002 *Journal of Forensic Sciences* paper and later commentary). Standing: **unproven retrospective theory**, not a 1992 diagnosis and not an excuse.
- **“He wanted a living mannequin.”** Dahmer described trying to create permanently submissive partners by injecting acid or boiling water into the brain. Standing: **his own confession**, medically fatal, not a successful technique.

### OPEN QUESTIONS

- What, if anything, would have happened if officers had run Dahmer’s name on May 27, 1991?
- Why were home visits waived for a child-sex offender in a high-crime building, and who formally approved that waiver?
- How many missing-person reports were deprioritized because of race, sexuality, or transience — a qualitative claim families made, not a complete audited count in this dossier?
- Exact early-press vs. final evidence inventory (heads, photographs) still varies in secondary sources.
- Uncharged Tuomi killing: only Dahmer’s word plus absence of remains.
- Whether any additional victims exist beyond the 17 he named. He said that was all; no confirmed extras.
- Public still confuses “insane” with “hospitalized.” He was found sane and sent to prison.

### HUMAN COST & AFTERMATH

Seventeen families lost sons, brothers, fathers. Children were left without fathers (Courtia Beanland; Emmanuel Lacy; Bradehoft’s three children; Lindsey’s daughter). The Sinthasomphone family was harmed twice. Milwaukee’s Black, Lao, Native, and gay communities absorbed both the murders and the police response. Officers returned to work after reinstatement. The apartment site was razed and never became a memorial garden. Families later watched their grief turned into streaming entertainment. Lionel spent the rest of his life answering for his son; David disappeared from public life. Dahmer was murdered in prison at 34; that neither healed nor satisfied many relatives.

### MYTHS VS REALITY

- **Myth:** He was found not guilty by reason of insanity. **Reality:** Guilty plea; sanity trial; found sane; prison.
- **Myth:** Police never entered the apartment until July 22. **Reality:** They walked Konerak inside on May 27.
- **Myth:** Only two officers were involved May 27. **Reality:** Porubcan was a third; firefighters also responded.
- **Myth:** He killed only in Milwaukee in the 1990s. **Reality:** First murder Ohio 1978; then a nine-year gap; Wisconsin 1987–91.
- **Myth:** He was charged with all 17. **Reality:** 16 convictions; Tuomi uncharged.
- **Myth:** Wisconsin could have executed him. **Reality:** No death penalty.
- **Myth:** Netflix is a documentary. **Reality:** Dramatized series; families objected.
- **Myth:** Victim names do not matter. **Reality:** They were workers, students, dancers, a deaf man visiting family, children.

Video Title: The Night Milwaukee Police Walked a Boy Back to Jeffrey Dahmer

Video Topic / Case Name: Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer; 17 murders (1978–1991); 16 convictions; Milwaukee sanity trial 1992; killed in prison 1994.

Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer was born in Milwaukee on May 21, 1960, to chemist Lionel Dahmer and Joyce Flint. The family settled in Bath Township, Ohio. He collected animal bones; his father taught him to bleach them, thinking it was science. By high school he was drinking in daylight. In spring 1978 his parents’ marriage collapsed. Joyce left with younger son David. Jeffrey, 18, stayed in the empty house.

On June 18, 1978, he picked up Steven Hicks, 18, hitchhiking to a Chippewa Lake concert, took him home for beers, and killed him when Hicks wanted to leave. Bones went into the woods. Six weeks later he failed out of Ohio State (GPA 0.45). In 1979 he enlisted; he served as a medic in Baumholder, West Germany, and was honorably discharged in 1981 for alcohol. After a failed stretch in Miami Beach he was sent to his grandmother Catherine in West Allis, Wisconsin.

He worked nights mixing chocolate at Ambrosia from 1985. He was fined for exposure in 1982 and put on probation in 1987. On November 20, 1987, Steven Tuomi died in the Ambassador Hotel; Dahmer said he did not remember it; no remains; never charged. In 1988 he killed Jamie Doxtator, 14, and Richard Guerrero at the grandmother’s house. Ronald Flowers was drugged there and survived.

In September 1988 Catherine made him move. Two days after he took a 24th Street apartment he assaulted 13-year-old Somsack Sinthasomphone. He pleaded guilty in January 1989. ADA Gale Shelton wanted prison. Lionel begged for treatment. Judge William Gardner gave work-release jail and five years’ probation. Home visits were waived. Before sentencing, Dahmer killed Anthony Sears.

In May 1990 he moved to Oxford Apartments 213, 924 North 25th Street, $300 a month. He killed Raymond Smith/Ricky Beeks, Eddie Smith, dancer Ernest Miller, and David Thomas, a young father. Neighbors smelled rot. Manager Sopa Princewill heard freezer and fish stories.

In 1991 the pace increased: Curtis Straughter, Errol Lindsey, deaf visitor Tony Hughes. On May 26–27 he lured 14-year-old Konerak Sinthasomphone — Somsack’s younger brother, a coincidence Dahmer later said he did not know. Konerak escaped naked and drugged onto 25th Street. Sandra Smith, Nichole Childress, and others called 911. Officers Balcerzak, Gabrish, and Porubcan believed Dahmer’s story of a 19-year-old lover, walked the boy back inside, did not run the name, and radioed a joke about delousing. Glenda Cleveland called back. After they left, Dahmer killed Konerak. He then killed Matt Turner, Jeremiah Weinberger, Oliver Lacy, and Joseph Bradehoft. Ambrosia fired him in mid-July.

On July 22, 1991, Tracy Edwards escaped with a cuff on one wrist and flagged down Officers Rauth and Mueller. Polaroids in a drawer. A head in the refrigerator. Dahmer: “For what I did I should be dead.” Confession: 17 dead. Fifteen Wisconsin counts; Ohio added Hicks. Tuomi uncharged.

January 13, 1992: guilty but insane. The trial was only sanity. Berlin called it a cancer of the mind. Dietz said a man who hides bodies and lies to police is not legally insane. February 15: 10–2, sane. February 17: 15 life terms, 936 years before parole in the prosecutor’s math. Families spoke. Rita Isbell lunged, screaming she hated him. Dahmer said he wanted death, not freedom, that he had created a holocaust, that he was so very sorry. May 1, Ohio added a sixteenth life.

Officers were fired, then reinstated in 1994. The building came down in November 1992. In prison Dahmer was baptized. On November 28, 1994, Christopher Scarver beat him to death with a metal bar in an unsupervised gym detail. Joyce asked if everyone was happy. Catherine Lacy said the hurt was worse because he was not suffering as they were.

The men he killed had names, jobs, children, and people who waited by windows. In 2022 a Netflix series put those families through it again without asking. The vacant lot on 25th Street never became a garden.
