### VIDEO TITLE

The Black Dahlia: What Happened to Elizabeth Short

### VIDEO TOPIC / CASE NAME

The January 1947 murder of Elizabeth Short (posthumously known as the Black Dahlia) in Leimert Park, Los Angeles, California — one of the oldest and most famous unsolved homicides in Los Angeles County. (Source: FBI History; HISTORY; Wikipedia pointer to primary reporting)

### ONE-SENTENCE LOGLINE

On the morning of January 15, 1947, a mother walking her child found the drained, bisected body of 22-year-old Elizabeth Short in a vacant Los Angeles lot; despite hundreds of officers, taunting letters, over 150 suspects, and decades of theories, no one was ever charged and the case remains open. (Source: FBI; HISTORY; Los Angeles Times / Harnisch)

### WHY THIS STORY WORKS

- Core emotion / hook: A young woman from Massachusetts ends up as a postwar American myth — nicknamed by newspapers, distorted by rumor, and never given justice. The mystery is both forensic and cultural.
- Why it fits a faceless documentary: Strong timeline, newspaper war, FBI fingerprint ID, taunting mail, cold case status, and named theories without needing graphic gore on screen.
- Visually depictable: 1947 Los Angeles vacant lot on South Norton Avenue, Biltmore Hotel lobby, Hearst newsrooms, manila envelopes with cut-and-paste letters, Sowden House exteriors, Mountain View Cemetery in Oakland, period streetcars and nightclubs.
- Suggested video length: 12–18 minutes.
- Differentiation: Focus on Elizabeth Short as a person, the documented investigation, and how myths (torture, prostitution, “infantile genitalia,” Hollywood stardom) diverge from autopsy and district attorney files — not another Ellroy/film retelling.

### CONFIRMED FACTS

- Elizabeth Short was born July 29, 1924, in the Hyde Park neighborhood of Boston, Massachusetts, the third of five daughters of Cleo Alvin Short Jr. and Phoebe May Sawyer. The family settled in Medford, Massachusetts. (Source: Wikipedia synthesis of birth/family records; Legends of America; Local Histories)
- After the 1929 crash, her father lost savings; in 1930 his car was found abandoned on the Charlestown Bridge and he was presumed dead. Phoebe raised the girls as a bookkeeper. He later resurfaced alive in California. (Source: Wikipedia; Legends of America)
- Short had bronchitis and severe asthma; after lung surgery around age 15 she spent winters in Miami for milder climate and dropped out of Medford High School during her sophomore year. (Source: Wikipedia; LA Times Harnisch 1997)
- In December 1942, age 18, she moved to Vallejo, California, to live with her father, then working at Mare Island Naval Shipyard. They argued; she left in January 1943. (Source: Wikipedia; Legends of America)
- She worked at the Base Exchange at Camp Cooke (now Vandenberg) near Lompoc; was arrested in Santa Barbara on September 23, 1943, for underage drinking; juvenile authorities directed her back east, but she returned to Florida. (Source: FBI; Wikipedia)
- In Florida she met Major Matthew Michael Gordon Jr.; she told friends he proposed after a crash injury in India; he died in a second crash August 10, 1945. (Source: Wikipedia)
- In July 1946 she relocated to Los Angeles to visit Lt. Joseph Gordon Fickling, stationed at Long Beach. She spent her last ~6 months in Southern California, working as a waitress and renting a room behind the Florentine Gardens on Hollywood Boulevard. She had no known acting credits. (Source: Wikipedia; HISTORY notes “Hollywood hopeful” framing)
- On January 9, 1947, after a trip from San Diego with 25-year-old married salesman Robert “Red” Manley, he said he dropped her at the Biltmore Hotel downtown so she could meet a sister visiting from Boston. Some accounts place her using a lobby phone; she was allegedly later seen at the Crown Grill Cocktail Lounge, 754 South Olive Street. (Source: Wikipedia; Local Histories; LA Times 1997)
- Her whereabouts between January 9 and discovery on January 15 — the so-called “missing week” — were never reliably established; alleged sightings were checked and ruled out. (Source: Wikipedia; Harnisch)
- On the morning of January 15, 1947, Betty Bersinger, walking with her child, found Short’s naked body in a vacant lot on the west side of South Norton Avenue between Coliseum Street and West 39th Street, Leimert Park. (Source: Novel Suspects; Wikipedia; HISTORY)
- The body was severed at the waist, drained of blood (skin appeared pallid white), washed, posed with hands over head and legs apart, with facial lacerations from mouth corners toward the ears (“Glasgow smile”). Medical examiners estimated she had been dead ~10 hours; death ~ evening of Jan 14 or early Jan 15. Little/no blood at the dump site indicated killing elsewhere. (Source: FBI; Wikipedia; Novel Suspects)
- Autopsy January 16, 1947, by Los Angeles County coroner Frederick Newbarr: height 5'5", weight 115 lbs, light blue eyes, brown hair, badly decayed teeth; ligature marks on ankles, wrists, neck; body bisected via technique resembling hemicorporectomy / transecting lumbar spine; cause of death cerebral hemorrhage / shock from facial lacerations and blows to the head. Sperm tests negative. (Source: Wikipedia summarizing Newbarr report; Newton Encyclopedia reprint references)
- Identification: fingerprints matched FBI files from her 1943 Santa Barbara arrest; FBI reportedly identified her within ~56 minutes of receiving Soundphoto fingerprints. (Source: FBI)
- Hearst’s Los Angeles Examiner contacted Phoebe Short in Boston, initially deceiving her that her daughter had won a beauty contest before revealing the murder — a journalistic episode later recalled with shame by Jim Murray. (Source: Wikipedia citing Harnisch interview with Murray)
- January 21, 1947: a caller claiming to be the killer phoned Examiner editor James Richardson, congratulated coverage, said he would eventually surrender, and told Richardson to expect “souvenirs of Beth Short.” (Source: Wikipedia)
- January 24, 1947: manila envelope to “The Los Angeles Examiner and other Los Angeles papers” with cut-and-paste text “Here is Dahlia’s belongings[,] letter to follow,” containing birth certificate, business cards, photos, papers, and an address book embossed Mark Hansen; packet cleaned with gasoline like the body. Partial prints compromised in transit to FBI. Same day, handbag and black suede shoe found near a garbage can ~2 miles from scene, also cleaned with gasoline. (Source: Wikipedia; Novel Suspects; A&E)
- Press nicknamed her “Black Dahlia” — linked by various accounts to The Blue Dahlia (1946), sheer black clothes, or a Long Beach drugstore nickname; DA investigators credited newspaper reporters (often Bevo Means). Early Herald-Express also used “Werewolf Murder.” (Source: FBI; Wikipedia; Harnisch)
- Mark Hansen (nightclub/theater owner; address book; Short had stayed at his home) and roommate Ann Toth’s claim Short rejected Hansen’s advances made him a suspect; he was cleared. Robert Manley was investigated and cleared after polygraphs. LAPD interviewed 150+ men. (Source: Wikipedia; A&E; HISTORY)
- ~750 investigators from LAPD and other agencies worked early stages (including sheriff’s deputies and California State Patrol). City Councilman Lloyd G. Davis posted a $10,000 reward. Many false confessions followed; some charged with obstruction. (Source: Wikipedia)
- Further taunting letters included a Jan 26 “Black Dahlia Avenger” note promising to turn in Jan 29 (no show) and a later paste-up: “Have changed my mind… Dahlia killing was justified.” (Source: Wikipedia)
- Based on precise cuts, police considered medical knowledge; mid-Feb 1947 they obtained USC Medical School student lists; background checks yielded nothing actionable. (Source: FBI; Wikipedia)
- By spring 1947 the case was largely cold. Sept 1949 grand jury examined LAPD homicide failures on women’s murders including Short’s; further tracing of Short’s past followed without a breakthrough. (Source: Wikipedia)
- Official status: never solved; no arrest for the murder; hundreds of confessions over decades (60 in initial investigation; 500+ later claimed). (Source: FBI; HISTORY; Wikipedia citing Sgt. John P. St. John)
- Short is buried at Mountain View Cemetery, Oakland, California. California’s early sex-offender registration push was prompted in part by the case (Assemblyman C. Don Field’s Feb 2, 1947 bill). (Source: Wikipedia)

### INVESTIGATION, MEDIA & INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE

- LAPD led; FBI assisted with ID, records checks, interviews, medical-student inquiries, and failed fingerprint match on letter. (Source: FBI)
- Hearst papers drove saturation coverage; false “torture for hours” reports circulated while police allegedly allowed misdirection to protect true cause of death (cerebral hemorrhage). (Source: Wikipedia)
- Reward and confession wave complicated leads. (Source: Wikipedia)
- 1949 grand jury criticized homicide performance on women’s cases. (Source: Wikipedia)
- Media nickname eclipsed Short’s real name; she was framed as adventuress / Hollywood prowler regardless of waitress reality. (Source: FBI; Wikipedia)
- Case never produced a charge for Short’s murder. (Source: FBI; HISTORY)

### THEORIES

1. **Unknown sexual sadist / skilled dissector** — Classic police theory from cuts and posing. Support: bisection precision, washing, posing. Weakness: never identified. Standing: unproven default.
2. **Dr. George Hodel** — Steve Hodel et al. Support: surgical skill, surveillance quotes, proximity, other allegations in his life. Weakness: no charge; meeting with Short unproven per LAPD clearing narrative. Standing: popular, contested.
3. **Leslie Dillon (possibly with Hansen / others)** — Eatwell. Support: mortuary skill, period suspicion, motel story. Weakness: Harnisch alibi claim; no conviction. Standing: possible per some authors, disputed.
4. **Mark Hansen alone** — Motive from rejected advances. Support: address book, proximity. Weakness: cleared. Standing: weak officially.
5. **Robert Manley** — Last seen with her. Support: opportunity. Weakness: polygraphs, release. Standing: disfavored.
6. **George Knowlton (Janice Knowlton)** — Familial accusation. Support: none solid. Weakness: excavation empty; detective rejection. Standing: discredited by investigators.
7. **Cleveland Torso killer / Jack Anderson Wilson** — St. John late interest. Support: mutilation parallels. Weakness: original LAPD discounted; Wilson died. Standing: unproven.
8. **Police cover-up / corruption** — Eatwell and others. Support: LAPD scandals of era, grand jury discontent. Weakness: hard to prove specific Dahlia cover-up. Standing: speculative.

### SCRIPT-READY RESEARCH PACK

**Video Title:** The Black Dahlia: What Happened to Elizabeth Short  
**Case:** Murder of Elizabeth Short, Los Angeles, January 1947 — unsolved.

Elizabeth Short (b. July 29, 1924, Boston; raised Medford, MA) was 22 when her drained, washed, bisected body was found the morning of January 15, 1947, in a vacant lot on South Norton Avenue in Leimert Park by Betty Bersinger. Ligature marks, facial lacerations creating a Glasgow smile, and precise bisection led police to suspect medical skill. Coroner Frederick Newbarr’s Jan 16 autopsy listed cause of death as hemorrhage/shock from head and facial trauma. Little blood at the scene meant she was killed elsewhere. The FBI identified her in under an hour from 1943 Santa Barbara underage-drinking fingerprints.

Last confirmed companion: Robert “Red” Manley, who said he left her at the Biltmore on January 9 after a San Diego trip. The missing week is blank. On Jan 24 someone mailed gasoline-cleaned personal papers and Mark Hansen’s address book to the Examiner after a taunting call. Hansen and Manley were investigated and cleared. 150+ men questioned; ~750 officers involved early; $10,000 reward; dozens then hundreds of false confessors. Taunting “Black Dahlia Avenger” letters promised surrender then withdrew. Case cold by spring 1947; 1949 grand jury criticized LAPD. 1950 DA surveillance of Dr. George Hodel produced controversial quotes; no murder charge. Later theories: Steve Hodel’s father, Leslie Dillon (Eatwell), Knowlton, Cleveland Torso links — all unproven. Myths (starlet, prostitute, hours of torture, congenital sex inability) conflict with stronger records. She is buried in Oakland. The FBI still calls the murderer unfound.
